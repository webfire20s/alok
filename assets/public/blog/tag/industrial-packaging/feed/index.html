Long celebrated for its purity, elegance, and sustainability, glass is fast becoming the go-to choice for businesses aiming to deliver premium products with a reduced environmental impact. As eco-friendly packaging gains momentum across industries, companies are turning toward this transparent solution not only for its environmental benefits but also for its brand-enhancing appeal.

However, transitioning to glass packaging is not a simple swap. It involves careful consideration of cost factors, operational changes, and long-term strategy.

This article unpacks the real cost of adopting premium, sustainable solutions like glass containers for businesses of all sizes. Rather than just crunching numbers, we’ll explore the theoretical underpinnings of cost, the variables that influence it, and why a comprehensive understanding is key to making informed decisions.

Whether you’re a small artisanal brand or an established manufacturer, knowing the actual cost drivers involved in switching to this format can be the difference between a successful upgrade and a financial burden. With the rise of eco-conscious consumerism and global regulatory shifts, it’s time to take a closer look at the full picture.

1. Why Businesses are Considering Glass Packaging

Opting for durable, reusable glass containers isn’t just a visual refresh—it reflects a broader shift in how companies want to be perceived. More and more businesses are leaning into glass because:

  • Sustainability is no longer optional – Consumers demand eco-conscious packaging, and glass is endlessly recyclable, aligning well with zero-waste and circular economy initiatives.

  • Premium positioning – It adds perceived value, elevating the product’s identity through tactile and aesthetic appeal.

  • Product protection – Unlike plastic, this material is non-reactive and impermeable, preserving quality and freshness—especially in food-grade and cosmetic applications.

  • Regulatory readiness – For sectors like food, pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics, glass meets stringent safety and compliance standards across global markets.

  • Consumer trust and nostalgia – This packaging evokes tradition, authenticity, and a sense of quality that resonates with both legacy and new-age consumers.

This growing shift is also fueled by global trends such as the rise of clean-label goods, artisanal offerings, wellness products, and export-focused brands. Glass becomes more than a design decision—it’s a business strategy that enhances storytelling and builds brand credibility.

It also supports transparency in labeling and supply chain ethics. Brands using glass often find it easier to convey purity and honesty, especially in an era where packaging speaks before the product itself.

2. Understanding the Types of Costs Involved

Transitioning to premium, rigid packaging requires a closer examination of multiple cost layers—some obvious, others hidden.

Production Costs

Manufacturing this material, though energy-intensive, results in unmatched clarity and purity. It involves melting raw components like silica sand, soda ash, and limestone to produce food-safe containers.

Energy efficiency improvements and the growing use of recycled inputs (cullet) are steadily reducing operational costs. Innovations in lightweight containers are also lowering resource usage without compromising strength.

Many suppliers now offer standard molds and scalable solutions to reduce tooling expenses and increase SKU flexibility. For example, Ajanta Bottle’s range of ready-to-use molds helps minimize entry costs for smaller businesses.

Transportation & Logistics

Rigid containers are becoming more logistically viable. Enhanced stacking design, cushioning techniques, and lightweighting innovations allow safer, more cost-effective shipping.

Because of their durability, these containers protect contents better in transit—especially in extreme temperature scenarios—reducing spoilage, leaks, and returns. Working with regional partners also reduces freight costs and delivery times.

Packaging vendors today often offer full-service logistics and supply chain management tailored to these materials, simplifying distribution and reducing handling complexity.

Storage and Warehousing

Modern designs are stackable, space-efficient, and compatible with pallet systems. Their inert nature ensures product freshness over long storage periods.

Unlike flexible alternatives, this packaging resists degradation over time. Optimized vertical storage and racking systems can help businesses reduce space requirements while improving stock rotation.

Industries such as gourmet foods, honey, ghee, and wellness products benefit significantly from this storage-friendly format.

Sustainability and Recycling Infrastructure

Few materials rival the environmental credentials of this option. It’s endlessly recyclable without loss of quality and is widely accepted across municipal and industrial recycling systems.

Brands adopting it also benefit from higher consumer trust, regulatory compliance, and retailer preference. Recyclable formats are prioritized by eco-conscious marketplaces, giving sustainably-packed products a competitive edge.

Refill stations, subscription models, and return-and-reuse systems are being adopted by forward-thinking companies to reduce waste and enhance customer loyalty—turning packaging into part of the brand experience.

3. Cost Myths vs. Reality

Let’s clear up some common misconceptions:

Myth: This packaging format is always more expensive.
Reality: While initial costs may be higher, its durability, reusability, and preservation benefits deliver better ROI over time.

Myth: Only premium brands can afford it.
Reality: SMEs are adopting cost-effective tactics like shared molds and hybrid material strategies to make it work.

Myth: It’s outdated.
Reality: Rigid containers today are innovation hubs—featuring tech add-ons like NFC and QR codes for traceability and engagement.

Myth: It doesn’t scale.
Reality: With standardized designs and low MOQs, businesses of all sizes can scale their rigid packaging cost-effectively.

Myth: This format lacks flexibility.
Reality: Modern vendors offer customization in tint, finish, and shape—meeting both aesthetic and functional needs.

When selected wisely, glass offers scalable potential across industries. Paired with the right strategy, glass can become a powerful brand differentiator. Many brands now view glass as more than just packaging—it’s part of the product experience.

4. The Size of Your Business Doesn’t Matter – Glass Costs Everyone Differently

The cost equation varies depending on business scale—but so do the strategic opportunities:

  • Startups can embrace limited-edition glass designs and small batches to appear eco-premium. Unique glass packaging builds shelf and social media appeal.
  • Mid-sized brands benefit from mold libraries and flexible order sizes from suppliers like Ajanta Bottle, enabling experimentation with glass formats without financial strain.
  • Large enterprises can justify upfront investment in custom tooling, implement circular logistics, and negotiate volume-based discounts. They often lead trends like refill models or biodegradable coating technologies.

The key is aligning glass packaging decisions with your brand mission, operational capabilities, and customer expectations. Smart use of glass containers becomes a tool for delivering both aesthetic appeal and functional strength—making packaging not just a necessity, but a strategic asset.

5. The Role of Glass Packaging in Pricing & Consumer Perception

Glass packaging isn’t just protection—it’s persuasion. It sends signals:

  • Purity and trust – Essential in food, cosmetics, honey, and wellness industries.
  • Luxury and care – Ideal for gourmet foods, craft beverages, and beauty products.
  • Sustainability and ethics – Attracting conscious buyers looking for plastic-free alternatives.

Studies show that packaging influences over 70% of first-time purchase decisions. A premium glass container can sway consumer choice in crowded aisles or scrolls.

It also shines on social media. Glass photographs beautifully, feels high-end in unboxing videos, and enhances a brand’s visual identity. As visual platforms drive buying behavior, premium glass packaging becomes an asset in storytelling.

Consumers are increasingly willing to pay more for eco-responsible materials. This makes the added cost not just an expense—but an investment in perception, retention, and long-term growth.

6. What Is a Cost-Benefit Analysis, Really?

A cost-benefit analysis (CBA) is a foundational tool for any business weighing a transition in strategy or materials. In the world of glass packaging, it means evaluating all costs against the tangible and intangible returns that this switch can deliver.

In the case of glass, a CBA includes:

  • Calculating Costs: Material sourcing, decoration, transportation, warehousing, and potential breakage handling.
  • Forecasting Benefits: Elevated brand image, shelf appeal, reduction in spoilage, regulatory compliance, and consumer loyalty.

Why CBA matters:

  • Helps forecast financial viability and budget planning.

  • Aids in setting long-term glass packaging strategy based on product lifecycle.

  • Provides clarity to stakeholders, board members, or investors.

It’s important not to overlook non-monetary benefits—improved consumer trust, media appeal, and export readiness can heavily influence long-term growth. Adopting glass is not just a material upgrade; it’s a branding and operational elevation that supports premium positioning.

7. Smart Ways to Offset the Higher Cost of Glass

Forward-thinking brands are already using the following approaches to reduce or balance the cost of premium packaging:

  • Lightweighting – Designing thinner yet strong containers to reduce both material and freight expenses.
  • Collaborative moulds – Sharing moulds across SKUs or with partners to save on tooling costs while scaling production.
  • In-house decoration – Ajanta Bottle offers in-house services like printing, frosting, and labelling, helping brands avoid third-party vendor costs.
  • Local partnerships – Sourcing from domestic suppliers can significantly cut down on shipping timelines and import-related costs.
  • Digital integration – Smart packaging elements like QR codes or augmented reality experiences can add value without increasing physical complexity.
  • Standardisation – Streamlining container sizes and closures across SKUs optimizes inventory and reduces complexity.
  • Refill models – Encouraging consumers to reuse premium containers supports a sustainable, circular business model.

Some brands also incorporate return incentives or collaborate with refilling stations—giving customers a reason to reuse packaging while keeping overall costs under control. These strategies turn containers into a long-term asset rather than a one-time expense—especially when the chosen material is glass, known for its reusability and visual appeal.

8. Future Outlook: Will Glass Become Cost-Efficient Over Time?

The answer increasingly leans toward yes.

  • Technology – Automation, energy-efficient kilns, and AI-driven design tools are making glass production more cost-effective.
  • Consumer demand – As demand for sustainable packaging continues to grow, bulk glass orders can help drive down per-unit costs.
  • Government support – Incentives and subsidies for recyclable materials are making glass packaging more attractive for environmentally conscious brands.
  • Supplier innovation – Lower MOQs, standardized moulds, and bundled services are simplifying access to high-quality glass containers.
  • Retailer pressure – Major platforms are pushing for recyclable options, giving glass-packed products preferential shelf space and marketing exposure.
  • Material science – New glass formulations are lighter and stronger, reducing breakage and transport costs while improving efficiency.

With policy support and technological advancements, the gap between glass and plastic is narrowing. Businesses that invest in premium, recyclable packaging today will be better positioned to lead tomorrow.

Conclusion

Glass may not always be the cheapest option—but it’s rarely just a cost. It’s a long-term investment in brand value, consumer trust, sustainability, and elevated product perception.

Whether you’re a boutique brand or a large FMCG manufacturer, the true cost of glass packaging depends not just on what you spend—but on how strategically you implement it. When done right, glass offers measurable returns—in both consumer loyalty and business growth.

]]> Glass Packaging in the Pharmaceutical Industry: A Trusted Solution for Quality and Safety https://www.ajantabottle.com/public/blog/glass-packaging-pharmaceutical-industry/ Fri, 10 Jan 2025 06:11:46 +0000 https://www.ajantabottle.com/blog/?p=3039 In the pharmaceutical industry, packaging is more than just a container—it’s a critical component that ensures product safety, efficacy, and compliance. Glass packaging has long been the preferred choice for pharmaceutical industry, offering unparalleled benefits that make it indispensable in the sector. In this blog, we delve into why glass packaging remains a cornerstone of pharmaceutical safety and innovation.

 

Why Glass Packaging Dominates the Pharmaceutical Industry

 

 

The pharmaceutical industry demands the highest standards of quality, safety, and reliability. Glass packaging meets these standards due to its unique properties, including chemical inertness, durability, and transparency. Here’s why it remains a leading choice:

  1. Chemical Inertness: Glass is non-reactive, meaning it doesn’t interact with the contents it holds. This property makes it ideal for sensitive pharmaceutical formulations, such as vaccines, biologics, and liquid drugs, where even minor contamination can compromise efficacy.
  2. Exceptional Barrier Properties: Unlike plastic, glass acts as an excellent barrier against oxygen, moisture, and other external contaminants. This helps maintain the stability and potency of the medicines, especially in long-term storage.
  3. Durability and Heat Resistance: Glass can withstand sterilization processes such as autoclaving, making it suitable for injectable drugs and other sterile pharmaceutical applications.
  4. Transparency: Clear glass allows for visual inspection of the product, enabling both manufacturers and consumers to assess the quality of the contents at a glance.

 

Applications of Glass Packaging in Pharmaceutical Industry

 

Glass packaging is used extensively across a wide range of pharmaceutical products. Common applications include:

  • Vials and Ampoules: Widely used for injectable medications, vaccines, and biologics.
  • Bottles: Ideal for syrups, suspensions, and oral liquid medications.
  • Cartridges and Syringes: Essential for pre-filled syringes and pen injectors in the growing biologics and insulin markets.

For instance, companies like Gerresheimer and Schott AG are global leaders in manufacturing pharmaceutical glass containers. They consistently innovate to meet the rising demands of the healthcare industry, including the production of Type I borosilicate glass vials for highly sensitive drugs.

 

Types of Glass Used in Pharmaceutical Packaging

 

Pharmaceutical glass packaging is categorized into three main types:

  1. Type I (Borosilicate Glass): Known for its superior chemical resistance and thermal stability, Type I glass is ideal for highly sensitive formulations, including injectable drugs and biologics.
  2. Type II (Treated Soda-Lime Glass): Treated to enhance its resistance to chemicals, Type II glass is commonly used for acidic and neutral aqueous solutions.
  3. Type III (Soda-Lime Glass): While less chemically resistant, Type III glass is suitable for non-aqueous solutions and dry products like tablets and capsules.

 

Sustainability of Glass Packaging

 

 

As the pharmaceutical industry aligns with global sustainability goals, glass packaging plays a pivotal role. Glass is 100% recyclable, making it an eco-friendly choice that reduces environmental impact. According to the Glass Packaging Institute, recycled glass can be endlessly reused without loss of quality or purity, supporting circular economy initiatives.

Moreover, major pharmaceutical companies are increasingly opting for lightweight glass bottles and vials to reduce carbon emissions during transportation. This shift demonstrates the industry’s commitment to balancing safety and sustainability.

 

Challenges in Glass Packaging for Pharmaceutical Industry

 

Despite its numerous advantages, glass packaging faces certain challenges:

  1. Breakage Risk: Glass is inherently fragile, posing risks during transportation and handling. However, advancements such as strengthened glass and improved packaging systems have mitigated this issue.
  2. High Cost: Glass packaging can be more expensive than plastic alternatives. Yet, its benefits in preserving drug quality and ensuring patient safety often outweigh the cost.
  3. Delamination: In rare cases, glass delamination—where glass flakes appear inside the container—can occur. Leading manufacturers now use advanced production techniques to minimize this risk.

 

Innovations in Pharmaceutical Glass Packaging

 

To address these challenges, manufacturers are driving innovations in glass packaging:

  • Smart Vials: These incorporate RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) tags for better traceability and anti-counterfeiting measures.
  • Coated Glass: Advanced coatings enhance the durability and chemical resistance of glass containers, reducing the risk of breakage and delamination.
  • Lightweight Glass: Reduced weight designs are gaining traction for their cost-effectiveness and eco-friendly appeal.

For example, Schott’s EVERIC® pure vials are designed to reduce delamination risk while maintaining superior drug stability. These innovations are paving the way for safer and more efficient pharmaceutical packaging.

 

Why Glass Packaging is the Future of Pharmaceutical Industry

 

The pharmaceutical industry continues to evolve, and so does its packaging. As drug formulations become more complex and patient safety becomes increasingly paramount, glass packaging remains the gold standard. Its unmatched properties make it indispensable in ensuring drug efficacy, safety, and longevity.

Furthermore, with the rise of biologics and injectable medications, the demand for high-quality glass containers is expected to grow significantly. According to a report by Grand View Research, the global pharmaceutical glass packaging market is projected to reach $6.47 billion by 2028, expanding at a CAGR of 6.2%.

Conclusion

 

Glass packaging remains a cornerstone of the pharmaceutical industry, combining safety, sustainability, and innovation. From vials and ampoules to advanced smart packaging, glass continues to evolve to meet the ever-changing demands of healthcare. By choosing high-quality glass packaging, pharmaceutical companies can ensure their products are safe, effective, and aligned with consumer expectations.

Today’s consumers are not just looking for visually appealing products but also for packaging that offers functionality and convenience. As a result, functional packaging is leading the way in the future of colour cosmetics.

 

 

1. Built-in Applicators for Effortless Application

 

 

 

 

In a fast-paced world, convenience has become a priority for consumers, and built-in applicators are a great example of how packaging can simplify the beauty routine in colour cosmetics. Many colour cosmetic products now come with integrated applicators, such as lip glosses with precision brushes or compacts that include a sponge. This all-in-one design means consumers no longer need to carry separate tools or struggle to find the right applicator, making it easier to apply makeup on the go.

The convenience of built-in applicators also appeals to a wide range of colour cosmetics consumers, from busy professionals to travellers who need quick and easy solutions. For instance, a compact powder that includes a sponge applicator allows users to touch up their makeup effortlessly throughout the day, while a lip gloss with an integrated brush provides a smooth and precise application in seconds. This added functionality enhances the overall experience and makes these products more appealing to time-conscious users.

Incorporating built-in applicators into colour cosmetics packaging design also offers brands an opportunity to differentiate themselves in a crowded marketplace. As more consumers seek efficiency in their beauty routines, brands that prioritize user-friendly packaging for their colour cosmetics are more likely to capture attention and loyalty.

Traditionally, colour cosmetic products such as lipsticks, compacts, and eyeshadow palettes relied on snap closures or twist mechanisms. While functional, these methods can sometimes lead to loose or broken closures, causing the product to spill or dry out.

Magnetic closures offer a solution that combines both security and style for colour cosmetics. These closures ensure that the product remains tightly sealed, preventing any unwanted spills or messes inside makeup bags or purses. Beyond practicality, magnetic closures add a touch of elegance to the packaging, offering a satisfying “snap” when the product closes—a small detail that can enhance the overall luxury of the user experience.

The smooth, sleek design of magnetic closures is also a draw for consumers who appreciate minimalistic and sophisticated packaging in colour cosmetics. For example, a lipstick with a magnetic cap not only looks stylish but also feels more secure in the user’s hands. This innovation in colour cosmetic packaging design goes beyond aesthetics, providing a functional benefit that consumers appreciate and trust.

Sustainability is no longer a niche concern—it’s a priority for consumers across all industries, including colour cosmetics. As environmental awareness grows, so does the demand for more eco-friendly packaging options. Refillable packaging is one such innovation that is gaining traction in the colour cosmetics world.

Refillable options allow consumers to purchase a product once and simply buy refills when the product runs out. This approach not only reduces waste but also helps consumers feel more connected to their colour cosmetics purchase. For example, a refillable compact or lipstick case can be used for years, with only the inner product being replaced. This shift towards sustainable packaging is in line with the growing preference for eco-conscious consumption, particularly in colour cosmetics.

For colour cosmetic brands, offering refillable packaging is an excellent way to show commitment to sustainability while fostering brand loyalty. Consumers who invest in a refillable colour cosmetic product are more likely to return to the brand for refills, creating long-term relationships. Furthermore, refillable packaging often uses higher-quality materials, reinforcing the idea that sustainability and luxury can coexist in the colour cosmetics segment.

In the age of personalization, consumers are increasingly looking for colour cosmetic products that reflect their individual tastes and preferences. Customizable packaging allows beauty brands to offer consumers a more personalized experience by giving them the option to choose or design their own packaging. Whether it’s selecting the colour of a compact case, adding an engraved name, or choosing a unique design for lipstick holders, customization enhances the user’s connection with the product.

Customization in colour cosmetics isn’t just about aesthetics—it also extends to functionality. Some brands allow users to mix and match different components of their packaging, such as selecting a specific applicator for a makeup brush or choosing a particular finish for their compact case. This is particularly important for products that are perishable, such as dairy products, juices and sauces. Lug caps, also known as twist-off caps or screw-on caps, provide a secure and reliable sealing solution for jars.

Lug caps are also efficient for use in high volume packaging operations. They can be applied and removed quickly, which can help to speed up the packaging process and increase productivity. This is particularly important in the food and beverage industry where speed and efficiency are often key factors in determining the success of a product.

Lug caps are also easy to open and close, making them convenient for the consumers.

Primarily, two types of lug caps are used in the food industry: Plain and checkered lug.

Plain lug caps and checkered lug caps are two types of lug caps that are used on food containers.

Plain lug caps are simple, smooth-sided caps that screw onto the container. They have a raised ring (lug) on the inside of the cap, which locks the cap and container together to ensure a tight seal, which helps to keep the food inside fresh. These caps are simple and easy to use, and are suitable for a wide range of products.

Checkered lug caps, also known as safety lug caps, have a raised, ridged design on the outside of the cap. This design provides a better grip, making it easier to open and close the cap. In this article, I will discuss the 5 best practices for using lug caps on glass jars in order to achieve the best possible results.

  1. Choose the right size lug cap for your glass jars

It is important to use a lug cap that fits the size and shape of your jar. A lug cap that is too small may not provide a proper seal, while a lug cap that is too large may be difficult to twist on and off. It is also important to consider the neck finish of the jar, as lug caps are designed to fit specific neck finishes. For example, a lug cap with a 70 mm diameter may fit a jar with a 70 mm neck finish, but may not fit a jar with a different neck finish.

 

2. Use a lug cap with the proper sealing mechanism

There are several types of lug cap available, each with a different sealing mechanism. The most common sealing mechanisms are gasket seals, foil seals, and induction seals. Gasket seals use a rubber gasket to create a tight seal around the jar neck. Foil seals have a layer of foil that is peeled back to expose the opening in the cap. Induction seals use a layer of aluminum foil that is bonded to the cap and sealed to the jar neck using induction heating.

3. Properly apply the lug cap to the glass jars

To properly apply a lug cap to a jar, first clean the jar neck and the inside of the lug cap to remove any dirt or debris. Next, align the lug cap with the jar neck and twist it on until it is secure. Avoid overtightening the lug cap, as this can cause the jar to break or the lug cap to become difficult to remove. If using a lug cap with a gasket seal, make sure the gasket is properly seated in the cap and not twisted or damaged.

 

4. Store glass jars with lug caps properly

To ensure the best possible sealing performance, it is important to store jars with lug cap properly. Avoid stacking jars on top of each other, as this can cause the lug cap to become misaligned or damaged. Instead, store jars in a single layer on a flat, stable surface. Avoid storing jars in extreme temperatures, as this can cause the lug caps to expand or contract and affect the seal.

5. Inspect lug caps regularly

It is important to regularly inspect lug cap for any signs of wear or damage. Look for cracks or deformities in the lug caps or gasket, as these can affect the sealing performance. If a lug cap shows signs of wear or damage, replace it to ensure a proper seal.

  5. Cylindrical: A straight-sided bottle with a round cross-section, often used for juices, syrups, and sauces.
  6. Taper: A bottle that narrows gradually from the base to the neck, often used for wine and spirits.
  7. Hourglass: An inverted taper shape, wider at the middle and narrow at both ends, often used for sauces and condiments.
  8. Bordeaux: A classic wine bottle shape, with a rounded shoulder and a straight or slightly angled body. Example: Château Margaux Wine Bottle.
  9. Oblong: These bottles often have a long and narrow shape, allowing for a comfortable grip and easy pouring.

Lets us now explore some common mistakes that business owners make when choosing a glass bottle shape and how to avoid them. This article will mainly cover round and square shaped glass bottles.

1. Not considering the product type: 

One of the most common mistakes business owners make is not considering the type of product they are packaging when choosing a glass bottle shape. Different product types may require different shapes of bottles.

For example, a round bottle may be best for packaging a beverage, while a square bottle may be best for packaging a food item. It is important to consider the product type and the packaging requirements before choosing a glass bottle shape.

2. Ignoring labeling requirements: 

The shape of the bottle can impact the amount of labeling space available. Business owners should consider the labeling requirements for their product and choose a glass bottle shape that provides enough space for labeling without sacrificing stability.

3. Not considering product stability:

The stability of the product within the bottle is another important factor to consider when choosing a glass bottle shape. Business owners should choose a glass bottle shape that provides enough stability to prevent the product from spilling or tipping over.

4. Ignoring storage requirements:

 The shape of the bottle can also effect the efficiency of storage. Business owners should consider the storage requirements for their product and choose a glass bottle shape that can be stored efficiently, such as a square bottle that can be stacked.

5. Choosing a novelty shape for the wrong reasons:

Specialty glass bottle shapes, such as a wine bottle or a beer bottle, can be an effective way to differentiate a product from the competition. However, business owners should avoid choosing a novelty shape for the wrong reasons, such as because it looks cool or unique, without considering the impact it may have on the product’s stability, branding, and labeling.

6. Underestimating the importance of bottle strength: 

The strength of the bottle is critical to ensure that it can withstand the demands of the packaging process, shipping, and storage. Business owners should choose a bottle shape that provides sufficient strength and stability to prevent breakage and protect the product. Generally, a round bottle helps equalizes pressure across its surface. On the other hand, due to uneven pressure, square shaped bottles have a high probability of getting cracks at corners.

7. Ignoring compatibility with automation and conveyor handling: 

Automated packaging and conveyor handling can increase efficiency and reduce costs, but not all bottle shapes are compatible with these systems. Business owners should choose a bottle shape that is compatible with the automation and conveyor handling equipment they plan to use, to ensure smooth and efficient operations. Machine filling of a product in round shaped bottles is much faster and efficient compared to square ones.

8. Not considering bottle orientation:

The orientation of the bottle during handling and packaging can also impact its compatibility with automation and conveyor handling. Business owners should choose a bottle shape that allows for easy orientation, such as a round bottle that can be handled in any direction.

9. Neglecting Ergonomics: 

The ergonomics of the bottle, including its shape and size, can impact the ease and comfort of handling. Business owners should choose a bottle shape that is ergonomically designed and fits comfortably in the hand, reducing the risk of injury during packaging and handling.

10. Underestimating aesthetics considerations:

 When considering aesthetic considerations, the choice of a glass bottle shape is subjective and depends on the specific goals and brand image of the business. Some shapes may be better suited for certain products and industries, while others may be more visually appealing to certain audiences.

For example, square or rectangular bottles can convey a modern and contemporary look, while round or oval bottles can have a more traditional or classic appearance. Tall and slender bottles can create a sleek and elegant look, while short and stubby bottles can give a playful and fun vibe.

Ultimately, the best glass bottle shape in terms of aesthetics is one that aligns with the product and brand image, and appeals to the target audience. Business owners should carefully consider the visual impact of the bottle shape, as well as its practicality, and make a decision that balances form and function.

11. Overlooking cost:

The cost of the glass bottle can impact the overall packaging budget. Business owners should consider the volume of the product they are packaging, as well as any custom features or branding elements, when choosing a glass bottle shape. They should also consider the cost-effectiveness of the bottle shape and avoid choosing a bottle shape that is too expensive for the product volume.
